# Documents and meeting notes

Other long-form documents that explain the item. The item's own versions belong to the file and version history investigator.

## What this source contains

- Memos, proposals, and briefs
- Policies, procedures, and manuals
- Meeting notes and minutes
- Contracts, engagement letters, and scopes of work that set a constraint
- Reviews written after something went wrong
- Plans that set priorities

The reason is often written out at length here before the work starts.

## How to search it

1. **Keyword searches.** Try the item's name, the key terms, the author's name, and the terms a reader would use. Bound the dates if you know when the item changed.
2. **Read candidates in full**, not the preview. The rationale is often mid-document.
3. **Follow links and sub-pages.** Alternatives considered and appendices often sit one level down.
4. **Check meeting notes** from the weeks before the item changed.
5. **Search the author's own folder or notebook** if you can reach it.

## What good evidence looks like here

- A "Background", "Problem", or "Purpose" section that matches the item
- An "Alternatives considered" or "Options rejected" section
- A review that names the item as the response to a specific problem
- Meeting notes that record "we decided X because Y" in the same date range, with the same people
- A contract or policy clause the item has to satisfy

## Common pitfalls

- **Outdated documents.** A plan written before the work may describe something that changed. Cross-check against what was done.
- **Plan versus result.** The document says X and the item does Y. Flag the divergence. The synthesizer surfaces it.
- **Template filler.** A required "Why" section filled with generic text is not an answer. Look for specifics.
- **Unlinked documents.** The most relevant one may not be linked from anywhere. Broad keyword searches help.
- **Several drafts.** Find the one that was finalized or approved. Check dates.
- **Restricted pages.** If you can't open a page, note it as a gap.

## What to return

For each relevant document:
- Title and link or path
- Authors and last-updated date
- The motivation text (verbatim quote), with page or section
- Linked documents the synthesizer may need to cite
- Whether it was final or draft
